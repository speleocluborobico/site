---
title: History
---

In 2013 the Speleo Club Orobico celebrated its first 40 years of life, as well as its first 35 years of activity within the Club Alpino Italiano.  
It should be pointed out, however, that the Group was not born as SCO 33 years ago: its origins go back a good 5 years earlier. We are in far-off 1973, and a handful of men, pioneers for their time, decide to form a caving group with the aim of broadening knowledge of karst phenomena and of protecting that wonderful underground world, which all too often was stripped of its beauty only for it to end up as a sterile display in some showcase or in the gardens of some villa.  
It is 19 March 1973 and, on the initiative and strong drive of Gian Maria Pesenti, the Gruppo Grotte Val San Martino of Caprino Bergamasco is born, with its headquarters in the back room of the village hairdresser's!  
The Group begins its exploration activity using wire ladders (a method now abandoned) and kicks off a series of remarkable discoveries for the time, which encourage other people to join the project.  
Rocco Zambelli, at that time curator of the Cave Register at the Museo Civico di Scienze Naturali in Bergamo, and the geologist Guido di Marsciano, both formerly accomplished active cavers, provide the scientific foundations of speleology understood as research. The collaborative approach with other caving organisations across Italy and the thirst for knowledge took concrete form the following year in the participation in the inter-group expedition to the Tatra mountains in Poland.

![006_101_è_nato_il_Gruppo_1973_1a_uscita_alla_Busa_del_Boter.jpg](https://www.speleocluborobico.org/site/images/006_101_è_nato_il_Gruppo_1973_1a_uscita_alla_Busa_del_Boter.jpg)Unfortunately, after the initial enthusiasm, the members lose interest in the activity, and only the following year, with the contribution of Maurizio Pesenti, Gianni Beltrami, Andrea Parenti and Cesare Mangiagalli, do explorations resume. Some twenty new caves are discovered and explored in Val S. Martino, including two 70-metre shaft-chasms and an abyss 88 metres deep, with a spectacular 53-metre bell-shaped internal shaft.  
Thanks to exchanges with other local and international caving organisations, knowledge and techniques reach high levels, so much so that in 1975 the group takes part in what can be called the historic technical turning point of exploratory caving: not without doubts, but with very little resistance, wire ladders are abandoned – the first caving group in Lombardy to do so – in favour of single rope technique and new specialised equipment.  
These are years of great ferment, of intense exchange with members of Groups from all over Italy, years of considerable satisfaction, both in exploration and in culture: suffice it to mention the very first single-rope descent of the legendary Buco del Castello (BG), accomplished by a team of just three SCO Members.  
Other members join the group, Alberto Testa, Ezio Ceresoli and Fabio Bajo; the latter immediately proves to be a true originator and promoter of activity, so much so that he will make a name for himself through the countless initiatives undertaken both within the Group and at regional and national level, such as editing the first issues of the journal Speleologia, as well as succeeding Rocco Zambelli as curator of the Cave Register of the province of Bergamo.  
The following year friendships and collaborations are forged with the cavers of the Gruppo Grotte di Milano CAI-SEM, of the Gaen of Ponte Nossa (now Gruppo Speleo Val Seriana Talpe) and of the Gruppo Speleologico CAI Varese, and a multitude of caves are revisited, among them: the Buco del Castello, the Grotta Marelli, the Lacca del Betù, the abyss in Val Cadur, the Grotta Guglielmo, the Antro del Corchia and several abysses in the Grigna; in nearby Switzerland, almost 22 km of the more than one-hundred-kilometre Holloch are covered in a single push!  
We come to 1977: the group takes its new name and becomes the Speleo Club Orobico. Gian Maria Pesenti is chosen as its first president, for the following 2 years, and the official headquarters are moved to Bergamo, at the home of member Anna Paganoni, who would later become the only female president in the group's history.  
It is a year full of satisfaction: the prospecting trips on the Zucco d'Alben in Val Taleggio lead to the discovery and exploration of six new caves, and at the Buco del Castello a continuation is discovered in the Ramo Talpe leading to a terminal chamber, later climbed. The first issue of the newsletter-journal Ol Bus also sees the light, which has now reached, with mixed fortunes, issue no. 15.  
Unfortunately it is also an ill-fated year because of accidents underground: SCO volunteers were involved in the recovery of two injured people, one at the Omber (Bs) and one at the Remeron (Va); in the latter case the prompt intervention was not enough to save the life of one of the two cavers.  
In 1978 the group is busy organising the 1st Caving Course, held at the C.A.I. Bergamo headquarters, and since then organised every year under the aegis of the Scuola Nazionale di Speleologia of the C.A.I.; the bond between the two associations takes concrete form with the SCO joining the large family of the Bergamo Section of the C.A.I., with permanent headquarters in Via Ghislanzoni.  
In collaboration with the Gruppo Speleologico Lecchese of the C.A.I., the Abisso Campelli is re-explored, and the new depth reached turns out to be -461 m.  
The group grows, and with it its exploration activity: the first summer camp is organised at the Piani di Artavaggio; again in the Abisso Campelli the Ramo Nuovo (-483 m, a new Lombard record) and the Ramo dei Brividi are explored; on that occasion the Abisso sotto Cima di Piazzo (-110 m) is explored, but it is only in November, thanks to a remarkable drought, that two members of the Group manage to pass the first sump and explore about 160 m of cave, reaching a third sump: the resurgence below, the Lacca della Bobbia.  
But it will be in 1979 that the group achieves a true "technical performance": the first Italian single-rope descent of the Provatina abyss on Greek soil, in the mountains of Epirus, a very deep shaft with a single vertical drop of no less than 392 m.  
However, alongside this great achievement the group meanwhile continues to bring home results on local ground as well: the Pilaf abyss (-113 m) is discovered and explored at the Piani di Artavaggio; the demanding Lacca sulla Cresta (-161 m) is explored by a mixed BG-MI-VA-LC team; and in addition an exploratory review with a complete geomorphological study of the Lacca del Berù of Parzanica is carried out. This work, so far unique of its kind in the Sebino area, would then be presented at the IX Congresso Lombardo di Speleologia in Lecco, together with an exhaustive study, likewise unique of its kind, on caving terminology in the dialects of the province of Bergamo.  
Meanwhile, the synergy with the CAI led to the appointment of one of our members to the newly founded Commissione Centrale per la Speleologia, with the result that our group was chosen by the Scuola Nazionale di Speleologia to organise the 2nd national refresher course for National Instructors, held in Roncobello. In the meantime the same member took part in the 1st National Course in "Single Rope" Technique at Monte Cucco and, the following year, in the exam and subsequent appointment as National Instructor, kicking off a series of National Courses held in the following years.  
In the 1980s, the harmony and liveliness of the group, enriched by new members such as Mario Trapletti and the "Gnomo" Rosi Merisio, would lead to the making of two films directed by member Federico Thieme, entitled "Ultra Limina" and "Ghnomus", true milestones of national and international cinematography, recognised above all with prestigious awards at international festivals in the field.  
These are also the years in which ecological cave clean-up campaigns are launched, supported by numerous educational initiatives for the protection of the delicate underground world.  
On the technical and exploration side, other great vertical drops are descended: Pozzo Revel (P.300 m), Aphanicè (P.328 m), Mafro Skiadi (P.354 m), Pot-2 (P.300 m); the bottoms of the legendary Gouffre Berger (-1,122 m), of the Spluga della Preta (-878 m) and of the Grotta di Monte Cucco (-890 m) are reached; the abyss il Tigre (-300 m) is explored in the Grigna; all crowned by two successive expeditions to Mexico in which two more legendary vertical drops are descended, again as Italian firsts: the Sotano El Barro (P.410 m) and the spectacular Sotano de las Golondrinas (P.333 m). Numerous cavities are also explored, of which noteworthy are La Cueva de Manuhantia and the Grutas de Llano Grande, both around 1,000 m in length.  
Again abroad, in the far north, an inter-group expedition is carried out and the through-trip of the Ragge Javre Raige cave is completed, with a spectacular exit onto a cliff face above the Norwegian fjord of the same name.  
Meanwhile, the Bus di Tri Fradei, later better known as the Grotta degli Orsi, is discovered and explored in upper Val Parina: the most important palaeontological site in the Bergamo area for its content of fossil remains of Ursus Speleus, which kept the Group's members busy for years with systematic excavation coordinated by the Museo Civico di Scienze Naturali of Bergamo.![22.1_cranio_di_ursus_spelaeus.jpg](https://www.speleocluborobico.org/site/images/22.1_cranio_di_ursus_spelaeus.jpg)  
These are the years of the discovery and exploration of the Grotta Europa in Vall'Imagna, an admirable example of subsequent educational use, given its beauty in terms of its formations.  
In the following 1990s, after a physiological phase of renewal, explorations continued with remarkable results throughout the Valle Imagna, in particular in the caves: Siberia, Alaska, Morti, Coren, Sbadol, Vuoto d'aria and Sorgente Carrera; in Val Brembilla in the caves Tamba di Laxolo and sorgente di Val Roncaglia; in Val Brembana, in the Dossena area, in the abysses: Lotto nord, Plastico, Abisso 13, Palme, Croasa del Culmen; in the Arera area, in collaboration with our friends of the Nottole group, in the abysses: Dolce Vita, Frank Zappa, Stratos; at the Piani di Artavaggio in the abyss W le Bambine and in the Grotta dell'Aragonite.  
Abroad, in Greece, on the Astraka and Stouros plateaus, in two separate summer camps, numerous predominantly vertical cavities were explored, among which the noteworthy abysses: Magik Jorgos, Pavlos and Francisco on the former; Trypa Limni Prassinos, Trypa Kristalia, Castore and Polluce on the latter, in addition to the discovery, in the field of biospeleological research, of 2 new species of troglobitic insects.  
With the aim of learning about and becoming familiar with important and imposing karst phenomena, several summer camps were held in France and Spain: in the Vercor near Grenoble, the legendary Gouffre Berger was descended again on two different occasions years apart, reaching its bottom (-1,122 m), and the Gouffre Gournier and the Tru qui Soufle were visited several times. In the Pyrénées-Atlantiques, not far from Lourdes (the best-known cave in the Western world), we covered several km of another legend, La Sima de la Piedra de S.Martin, and descended the Aphanicè again; also in the Atlantic Pyrenees, in the region of Cantabria in Spain, together with our caver friends from Valencia of the "la Senyera" Group, we completed the difficult and demanding through-trip of the Sima del Cueto (P.300 m) / Cueva Coventosa, for a total of 890 m of vertical range and 7 km of underground route; the "impressive" Cueva Canuela was also visited, in this case too for several km.  
At the beginning of the new millennium, certainly noteworthy also for its humanitarian side, a CAI Bergamo caving-mountaineering expedition to Kosovo is organised, with the important aim, fully achieved, of passing on a passion for the mountains in all its aspects to young Kosovars, survivors of the tragedy of the war lived through in those years.  
A caving course and a climbing course were held with excellent results, and a number of caves were also explored, later continued in the following years by our Kosovar friends, former course participants.  
Meanwhile, the commitment to the Scuola Nazionale di Speleologia led to the appointment of five more Instructors as well as a new National Instructor, but above all to our organising: a General Assembly of the School, two National Courses in Snow Science, two National Courses in Biospeleology, a Workshop on the use of GPS for cavers in Dossena, and a National Course preparatory to the Instructor exam held in Oltre il Colle.  
With the start of the new millennium, thanks to the commitment of members young and not so young, coordinated over these last 6 years by "son of a caver" Francesco Merisio, who then passed the baton to Marco Frassinelli, the SCO has continued, and still continues, the exploration research and outreach activities carried out so far: an admirable example is the exploratory review of the Abisso 13 in Dossena, which with great effort was taken from –110 m to –300 m in depth.  
Meanwhile, some members took part in two separate inter-group research campaigns in Mexico, bringing back remarkable exploration results such as the Cueva Mama Mia – Cueva del Viento karst system, explored and surveyed for a total length of over 7.5 km.  
At regional association level, our Group took part as a founding member in another historic event: the closure and dissolution of the Ente Speleologico Regionale Lombardo, to which it had actively contributed as a member over the years, and its refounding as the Federazione Speleologica Lombarda.  
  
We come to the present day: collaborations with regional and national groups and associations have led SCO Members to important explorations and research.  
With our friends of the Gruppo Speleologico Valseriana Talpe, the Abisso di Monte Leten (BG) was re-explored to a new depth of -400 m, but above all the exploration of the cave named "5 in Condotta", located in Val Canale, is still under way; it extends through galleries and shafts for over 5 km.  
Excellent results were also obtained at the Abisso sotto Cima di Piazzo at the Piani di Artavaggio (LC), with a new depth of –261 m reached.  
Again on the technical and exploration front, mention must undoubtedly be made of the participation of SCO Past-President Aldo Gira in no fewer than two exploration pushes in the Abisso W le Donne, which opens on the northern Grigna, explored on that occasion to two separate bottoms at –1260 m and –1313 m, as well as his participation in an Italian expedition to Uzbekistan, with the exploration of the Dark Star cave, over 900 m deep.

The most recent SCO exploration success, coordinated by the current President Francesco Merisio, was the exploratory review of the Laca a N del Passo Menna, long known for its first 15 m of length, and re-explored by the "Orobici" Members to a new depth of – 395 m.  
  
Meanwhile, awareness campaigns on respect for the underground world have continued, aimed above all at children and young people and, not least, at more or less occasional cave visitors. Particular mention should be made of the effort put in on 27 and 28 September 2008 at the Buco del Castello in Roncobello, with the clean-up of one of the most visited caves in Lombardy, as well as the latest campaign at the Grotta La Ena, located in the territory of Caprino Bergamasco, a cavity used for decades as an illegal dump, all as part of the national event "Puliamo il buio".  
But the most heartfelt commitment, in which the greatest hopes for growth are placed, is above all the Introductory Caving Course, always held every year under the aegis of the Scuola Nazionale di Speleologia CAI, both because it is the ideal means of bringing people closer to one of the last frontiers of the unexplored and the unknown, and because it is a well-tested way of meeting new friends who bring momentum and vitality to an exciting activity full of surprises, one that has always been able to create a bond of strong friendship but also of close debate among the Members of the group.
