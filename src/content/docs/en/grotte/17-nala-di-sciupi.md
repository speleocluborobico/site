---
title: Nala di Sciupì
--- 
**LoBg 1245**

The cave has always been known to the locals thanks to its wide doline-and-slope entrance, but the first exploration (using ladders) appears to be credited to the Gruppo Speleologico Bergamasco, which later merged into the G.S.B. Le Nottole. The research was coordinated by Rocco Zimbelli, who also attempted a dye trace with chemical dyes, which emerged in the Büs de Algàse below.

For some years strange rumours circulated about phantom voice or even physical connections between the two caves, helped also by the lack of a survey of the Nala di Sciupì.

In 1989 Le Nottole decided to survey the cave while at the same time trying to extend it by working on the small terminal sump. The hard (and noisy) digging work made it possible to bypass the small sump from above, only to drop into a flooded little chamber of insignificant size. Among other things, on that occasion it was concluded beyond doubt that it was impossible for anyone in the Büs de Algàse to hear any sound coming from Sciupì. Climbing an aven (Risalita del figo d'India) located before the flooded chamber leads to a small decorated chamber containing a fine cave pearl 18 mm in diameter (now unfortunately stolen), while higher up an ascending squeeze enters a high-level meander (Meandro retroverso); this traverses over a P5 topped by a large aven and continues, high and narrow, rising gently until it closes down in a fracture after about 40 metres. Climbing the upper aven you reach a chamber called the Sala del Trivio, from which you can reach either a small decorated side branch or the "Risalita delle radici", a few metres below the surface.

At the beginning of 1994 the collaboration between SCO and Nottole led to the completion of the topographic survey of the cave. On that occasion another, fruitless, climb was attempted towards a significant water inlet connected to the entrance shaft (risalita dei fix sprecati).  
The final calculation gives about 400 m of real length, about 200 m of plan length and a depth from the entrance to the bottom of 92 m. The ascending branch returns practically to the same altitude as the entrance, starting from -80 m.

## Morphological notes

The entire cave (formed in the Dolomia Principale) develops along a large fracture oriented NNE, parallel to the course of the Büs de Algose, but it runs in the opposite direction. It can be assumed that the hydrological connection occurs through a saturated zone at an altitude of 850 m a.s.l. (the entrance of the Büs de Algose is at the same altitude); in plan the two bottoms are about 200 m apart.  
A doline about 20 m in diameter leads, via a slope full of rubbish, to the cave entrance (942 m a.s.l.) and after a few metres to the majestic 70 m shaft that characterises it. Traversing high up, inside the meander, it is possible to rig the entire vertical in a single pitch; alternatively there are several rigging options, all spectacular and necessary, since in the event of heavy rainfall the spray could cause serious breathing problems.  
At the bottom of the shaft you enter an active small meander studded with mushroom-shaped formations, which after a few metres leads to the chamber of the Risalita del Figo d'India (rope up on the right). The small meander continues downwards through awkward, wet passages, interrupted only by a 6 m pitch, to the lowest point of the cave. The risalita dei fix sprecati is not rigged, while the ascending branch is fully rigged but with equipment whose reliability is entirely unverified.
