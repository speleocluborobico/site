---
title: Grotta della Selva
---
**LoBg 3784**

Area – Bedulita Val Imagna  
The cave is in a senile stage of evolution: it is now completely fossil and contains considerable fills of both mud and flowstone. Despite its short length it can be divided into three parts: the entrance area, the intermediate area and the final area.

The entrance gallery consists of a joint 6 metres high, widened by corrosion to more than 2 metres across; the area has been affected by limited collapse phenomena which have exposed the sub-horizontal stratification. Nevertheless, the clasts are not abundant and are by now cemented together. If anything, there are formations, mostly stalactitic. The gallery ends after 20 metres at a flowstone cascade. Climbing this, you reach the intermediate area, a conduit parallel to the initial gallery but with phreatic features (rounded, smooth roof), partly filled by a thin layer of plastic clay (a few centimetres thick) covering a calcite substrate. There are few formations, apart from a niche heading towards the entrance gallery. Small pools have formed in the clay, and their floors have become coated with calcite. After 10 metres the cave turns sharply to the right and changes morphology once again; it now takes the form of a wide, low bedding-plane passage with ill-defined outlines, richly decorated with speleothems and with a thick layer of watery mud on the floor. On the left the small phreatic conduit continues towards the initial joint, but becomes almost impassable. In the bedding-plane section there was certainly once a small lake, now dried up, as shown by a hollow in the floor and by the numerous formations which, with the characteristic discs that form at the surface, record the different water levels. Finally, the gallery intersects three more joints that cut across its roof.

The cave lies very close to the Buco del Corno (about ten metres away "through the rock") and can be considered a continuation of its upper branch, with which it is morpho-structurally continuous. The upper branch of the Buco del Corno is in fact also a gallery developed alternately along joints and bedding planes, and it is likewise in a senile stage (fossil, with thick mud fills). The ancient small lake of the Grotta della Selva, and above all its phreatic conduit, bear witness to the former presence of a large quantity of water; this then flowed into the upper branch of the Buco del Corno and from there into the entrance chamber, where it perhaps mixed with the water coming from the younger active branch. After all, neither the present flow of the active branch nor its morphology seem to account for either the excavation of the chamber or its subsequent filling, so it seems necessary to assume a substantial inflow of water from the upper branch.  
The Grotta della Selva lies in a fossil level and the water supply is limited to a faint dripping that produces the formations. Moreover, the total lack of draught means that the cave is saturated with humidity, and the lack of evaporation allows the few drops of water to keep the mud in the bedding-plane gallery extremely wet and the level of the pools in the phreatic tube high.
