---
title: Grotta Europa
---
**LoBg 3705**

The Europa, one of the most beautiful caves in the valleys of Bergamo, is located in Valle Imagna in the municipality of Bedulita; it is reached by driving past the village towards Costa Imagna.

After a couple of km you come to a hairpin bend on a ridge, where you leave the car. Here, heading west, you take an old cart track, sloping gently downhill, which after 70 m leads to a metal gate, the entrance to an old quarry. Passing it at the side, you continue to an uninhabited house and carry on to the right along a path through beech and birch trees.

After 300 m the path turns 90 degrees to the left, taking us into the small valley; we continue for another 200 m, crossing a small tributary stream on the left that runs across the path.

We leave the path (going straight on, after 100 m the path ends at the mouth of the Buco del Corno with its wide entrance) and follow the tributary upstream for 200 m to reach the foot of a rock face where the stream forms a small 2-metre waterfall. Higher up on the right, beneath a natural overhang, is the entrance to the Grotta Europa.

The entrance measures about 100x40 cm, and cold air blows out of it. You proceed horizontally for 6/7 m (the passage widens up to 2 m), after which the roof gradually rises to one metre over a length of 3 m. Keeping to the right, you climb the one-metre step (after removing the padlocked bar) and through a 2 m squeeze you reach the large chamber.

The chamber was made accessible by enlarging the squeeze, a job that took about fifteen nights and was completed on the Sunday of the 1986 European Parliament elections; hence the name Grotta Europa. The large oval chamber measures 30 m long by 20 wide and 10 high; from the centre of the dome a small perennial waterfall flows out, its water disappearing on the right-hand side of the cave. Also beside the waterfall stands an enormous, stocky stalagmite. The cave is rich in speleothems of every kind, straws, curtains and columns, not to mention helictites and stalagmites; at the far end of the chamber there are also huge flowstone cascades up to 5 metres high, running from the roof down to the floor.

The stream created by the waterfall poses no problems; the water is no more than 20 cm deep and you follow it for 3-4 m, after which you find yourself in the straws area; from here you generally continue to the left, while the cave carries on in the same direction for another 30 m. To access this branch you need to reach the column at the end of the straws area, then move right against the wall and enter the 2 m long squeeze.

You continue through the chamber, passing some stalagmites no more than a metre high, until you reach the large flowstone cascades. You then pass through a small gorge that leads to an active rimstone pool, where the calcium carbonate in the water helps to build up its rim. Along the sides, underwater, there are other formations typical of this kind. The pool is fed by a small jet of water which at the same time drills into and builds up a stalagmite. Passing the pool on the left, through a narrow passage, you reach a small fossil lake of about 2x2 m. Here in the niche you can find more formations, stalactites and curtains.

The waterfall is on our left and we have climbed about 3 metres above the stream.

You then continue for four metres along a ridge, paying attention to the side recesses, until you reach the starting point where, in the past, the remains of carnivorous animals were found. The way out, going downhill, is taken head first, letting yourself slide slowly out of the squeeze.

NOTE: The Grotta Europa is one of the most beautiful caves in the Bergamo area, with a variety of speleothems that make it extremely interesting from a geomorphological point of view. Unfortunately, because of its location and easy access, in recent years the cave has suffered quite a few acts of vandalism: therefore in 2009 the municipality of Bedulita decided to issue regulations for entry in order to safeguard the cave's formations and protect them from ill-intentioned visitors.
